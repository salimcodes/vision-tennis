Gymnastics: Fujitsu and the FIG have built the Judging Support System since 2017. Partial use at the 2019 Worlds and Tokyo 2020. At the 2023 Worlds in Antwerp, where Biles competed, it went live on all 10 apparatuses. It is camera-based (it used to be lidar) and acts as a "second opinion" for borderline difficulty scores.

Tokyo 2020: Intel's 3D Athlete Tracking (3DAT) used standard video, pose estimation and no body sensors on the 100m, 200m, relays and hurdles. It produced top-speed and deceleration overlays for broadcast replays.

01 · Football · Qatar 2022

Offside, in seconds.

12 cameras29 body pointsball @ 500 Hz
SAOT: 12 roof cameras track 29 points on every player. A sensor inside the ball sends data 500×/s to find the exact kick point. The AI raises an offside alert and the VAR confirms it. FIFA estimated decisions would drop from about 70s to 20–25s.
02 · Cricket · DRS

Where would it have gone?

Cricket: Hawk-Eye ball tracking reconstructs the ball's path from multiple high-speed cameras and predicts it beyond the pad for LBW reviews. Players can challenge on-field calls through the Decision Review System.
03 · NBA · since 2023–24

Players, as skeletons.

14 cameras29 points / player
NBA: Sony's Hawk-Eye replaced Second Spectrum's "dot" tracking in 2023–24. Each player went from one 2D point to 29 3D body points plus the ball, in near real time. The league has said it aims for automated out-of-bounds and goaltending calls in future seasons.

F1: at the 2023 Austrian GP, four officials had to process about 1,200 potential track-limit breaches. The FIA then trialled "Computer Vision" at Abu Dhabi 2023, using shape analysis to count pixels beyond the white line and filter real breaches (all four wheels over). Note: this is a line-crossing problem, which sets up the tennis section.
05 · Gymnastics · Fujitsu × FIG

Joint angles, judged.

JSS: markerless skeleton recognition builds a 3D model of the routine and matches it against the Code of Points. It is used on all 10 apparatuses from the 2023 Worlds.

Line calling is solved: live electronic line calling was first trialled at the 2017 Next Gen ATP Finals. The Australian Open went fully electronic in 2021, followed by the US Open. The ATP made it tour-wide from 2025, and Wimbledon dropped its roughly 300 line judges in 2025 after 148 years. It uses about 12 cameras per court and makes a call within about 0.1s. So the line question is closed and the serve question is next.
Problem 2 · Which serve?

Still
open.

Every point
starts here.
Why serve type matters: the serve is the one shot fully under the server's control, and it starts every point. If you can automatically tag each serve's type (and its placement), you get each opponent's habits: which serve on break point, which one out wide on the deuce side. That is scouting. Today it is done by hand from video, if at all. (The court graphic is illustrative, not real data.)

BlindTest (Rahmanzadehgervi et al., ACCV 2024): GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5 Pro and Claude 3/3.5 Sonnet scored only about 41–76% at counting whether two simple line plots intersect 0, 1 or 2 times. A human gets 100%. (The figure shown is a drawing in the same style, not from the paper.)

THETIS (Gourgari et al., CVPR Workshops 2013, NTU Athens) is the largest established tennis-action dataset. It has 12 shot classes from 55 beginner-to-intermediate players, recorded with Kinect (RGB, depth, skeleton; 8,374 clips total, 1,980 RGB). Players swing a racket with no ball, so there is no spin, no trajectory and no bounce. Exactly what tells serves apart is missing.
